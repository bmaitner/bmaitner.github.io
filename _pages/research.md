The Maitner Lab studies how biodiversity is distributed across the globe, what maintains it, and how we can best conserve it. We build larger and better biodiversity datasets and the computational tools needed to use them, then apply them to long-standing ecological questions and pressing conservation problems.

## Biodiversity Big Data and informatics

Ecology's biggest questions need data at global scales. We develop and maintain open datasets and software that bring together species occurrences, traits, and phylogenies, and we study the gaps and biases in those data. For example, socioeconomic and biogeographic factors jointly shape where plant trait data exist ([Maitner et al. 2022](/publication/2022-10-01-maitner_et_al)).

## Functional traits and community assembly

At local scales, we use functional, phylogenetic, and taxonomic approaches to understand how environmental and biotic factors assemble and maintain communities. We also develop better methods for estimating trait distributions, such as bootstrapping approaches that outperform community-weighted means ([Maitner et al. 2021](/publication/2021-08-01-maitner_et_al)).

## Macroecology and biogeography

We study the distribution of biodiversity across whole continents and the globe, asking how history, climate, and species interactions shape the patterns we see today.

## Invasions and global change

Why do some introduced species succeed while others fail? We showed that the phylogenetic structure of both source and recipient communities helps predict the outcome of bird introductions ([Maitner et al. 2022](/publication/2022-06-01-maitner_et_al)).
